3 · Three excerpts from the book

For each one: which chapter and figure, what the figure says, and what it means to me.

Excerpt 1 · Textbook Fig. 3.5

My explanation

Potential density cannot be read off temperature or salinity alone: on the same T-S diagram, the isopycnals shift when the reference pressure changes from 0 dbar to 4000 dbar. Parcel 1 has the same density as surface water; the two parcels labelled 2 are Mediterranean (saltier) and Nordic Seas (fresher) source waters — very different salinity, yet comparable density.
Excerpt 2 · Textbook Fig. 5.5

My explanation

Of the 100 units of incoming shortwave radiation, part is absorbed by the atmosphere and part is reflected back to space, leaving about 48 units reaching the surface; the ocean returns energy as longwave radiation, sensible heat and latent heat. This is exactly the surface-flux term of the mixed-layer heat budget.
Excerpt 3 · Textbook Fig. 7.13

My explanation

Wind-stress curl drives surface convergence and divergence, which stretches or squashes the water column; conservation of potential vorticity then pushes the column poleward or equatorward, producing the interior Sverdrup flow, closed by a western boundary current.

4 · Book ↔ class: same, different, extra

Same

Chapter 7's wave-speed formulas and the deep/shallow criteria (h > λ/2, h < λ/20) match the lecture, and westward Rossby propagation matches the β-effect section.

Different

The book treats each wave type in its own place; the lecture asked us to put them side by side on one set of four axes — an organising device added in class.

Extra

Internal waves were introduced only at the level of "waves inside a stratified fluid, larger interface displacements"; nonlinear internal waves were left to the book.

5 · Beyond the textbook

Sources, papers or datasets I looked up outside the book, and how they relate to the chapter.

Extension 1 · EGU27 microbe-interaction session (conference submission)
The textbook describes the physics of seawater; it does not tell you how physical oceanography intersects marine biology. I looked through the EGU session list and found that a microbe-interaction session can take physical processes (environmental gradients, stratification, mixing) as the physical backdrop for ecology, so I settled on that target and added a paragraph of environmental-gradient narrative to fit the abstract to the session.
Extension 2 · Three-piece science-graphics kit (data figure / schematic / Mermaid)
Outside the book, I turned figure-making into a tool: I downloaded three PPT-making sub-skills and used them to generate an HTML page (see "science skills kit" in the content list). It is not textbook material, but it makes "explaining a result clearly" repeatable.
